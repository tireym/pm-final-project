Future state journey: proving the card's value to large merchants

From skeptic to advocate in four stages. Riverty measures what the card changes at the merchant's checkout, so her answer to "show me what I get" comes from data.

Persona
Katrin, ecommerce lead at a large online retailer that offers Riverty at checkout and is being pitched on the new card.
Strategy
Riverty measures and shows the behavior change that proves the card improves the merchant's business.
Value proposition
Riverty builds stronger relationships with merchants and consumers by using its new banking license well.
  1. Connect

    User action

    Links checkout to Riverty dashboard → replaces manual data exports.

    Internal state

    Skeptical, asking "why you?" → needs proof before committing.

    Pain point addressed

    Manual exports and spreadsheets → one live view of Riverty results.

  2. Baseline

    User action

    Sets goals and a holdout group → isolates the card's true effect.

    Internal state

    Cautiously curious → willing to test with limited risk.

    Pain point addressed

    Cannot isolate card effect → credible before/after comparison.

  3. Prove

    User action

    Reads uplift report → sees incremental sales, basket size, repeat purchases.

    Internal state

    Convinced by her own data → can defend Riverty internally.

    Pain point addressed

    "Show me what I get" (N-08) → answered with evidence.

  4. Expand

    User action

    Activates partner offers (B5) → gives cardholders a reason to choose her checkout.

    Internal state

    Confident, owns the case → keeps Riverty prominent at checkout.

    Pain point addressed

    Card works anywhere, no reason to pick Riverty → offers drive loyalty.

Three competitive advantages over the manual workaround

  1. Full-picture dataRiverty sees card, pay later and partner spend. Katrin's own checkout data shows only her shop.
  2. Continuous, not one-offA live dashboard replaces her spreadsheet comparison, so proof stays current as behavior changes.
  3. Proof leads to actionMeasurement connects to partner offers (B5), so she can act on results, not only keep or drop Riverty.

Assumptions to validate: the unified card and pay later view depends on how the banking license lets Riverty combine and share this data, which needs legal and consent review. The Stage 2 holdout design is untested, and an employee-heavy pilot group (N-10) would skew early results. Sample data and fictional research references (N-08, N-10, B5) come from the practice data pack, not Riverty research.
